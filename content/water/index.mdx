# Water

Hyperreal© Water is one shader that covers the whole water problem: the
surface, the depth beneath it, and the view from below. Drop it on any mesh —
a plane, a river spline, a bathtub — and it works out where the bottom is on
its own. No volume to author, no second material, no setup scene.

## Depth, not transparency

Water colour comes from how far light travels through it, not from a flat
transparency setting. Shallows read warm and let the bottom through; the
channel goes deep and opaque; the transition between them is continuous.

## Caustics that stay put

Caustics play across whatever sits under the water, and they move with the
surface rather than sliding against it — the single most common tell in a water
shader. Subsurface scattering lights the backs of waves, which is what sells
thickness on a crest with the sun behind it.

## Underwater, out of the box

Cross the surface and you're underwater. It's the same material, with its own
set of controls — nothing to script, nothing to trigger, no second shader to
keep in sync.

## Built to be cheap

Water is written to run on modest hardware, including the Mobile renderer.
Work that can't be seen isn't done: detail that has no effect at distance is
skipped, and effects fade out before they stop earning their cost.
