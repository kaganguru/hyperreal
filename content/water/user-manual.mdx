# Water User Manual

## Setting up

Assign the Water material to any mesh — a plane, a river spline, a bathtub. It
finds the bottom on its own, so there's no volume to author and no second
material for the underside: a single mesh renders correctly viewed from either
side.

Water has no dependencies and runs on either renderer.

## Surface

Four independent layers of motion stack into the surface:

| Layer | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Swell | The large-scale silhouette of the water |
| Ripples | Mid-scale movement on top of the swell |
| Fine detail (×2) | Two grades of small-scale sparkle |

Each layer has its own **scale**, **speed** and **strength**, so you can tune
the silhouette of the water separately from its sparkle. One **master speed**
drives them all when you just want the whole thing calmer or angrier.

Every layer takes your own textures, so the character of the water is yours to
change. Tiling is broken up automatically — no visible repeat on a large body
of water, at any distance.

## Depth and colour

You control:

- **How fast** light is absorbed with depth.
- **Where** the absorption starts.
- **What colour** the water absorbs toward.

**Refraction** bends whatever is behind the surface. A **distance fade** keeps
a large lake looking like water at the horizon instead of dissolving into
shimmer.

## Caustics and subsurface

- **Caustics** — scale, intensity and wobble.
- **Subsurface scattering** — lights the backs of waves when the sun is behind
  a crest.

## Underwater

The underwater state switches on automatically when the camera crosses the
surface. It has its own controls:

- **Colour absorption with distance**, tunable per channel — the reason deep
  water goes blue-green.
- **Depth falloff**, tuned for looking outward rather than down.
- **Caustics intensity** on everything around you.
- **Refractive warp** across the whole view.
- **God rays**, with speed and density controls.

## Performance

<Callout type="info">
  Water is a screen-space effect: cost scales with how much of the frame it
  covers, not with how large the body of water is.
</Callout>

Detail that has no effect at distance is skipped, and effects fade out before
they stop earning their cost.

## Not included

So you know what you're buying: no screen-space reflections, no simulated wave
displacement, no wave-driven foam, and no impact or ripple-spawning system.
Waves are texture-driven, which is what keeps them cheap and art-directable.
